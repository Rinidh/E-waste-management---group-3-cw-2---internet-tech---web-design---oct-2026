Learning Hub

Everything you need to know about electronic waste — what it is, why it is dangerous, and how to handle it safely.

What Counts as E-Waste?

📱 Small Appliances

Mobile phones, chargers, kettles, toasters, remote controls, cables and small kitchen devices.

🖥️ IT Equipment

Laptops, desktop computers, monitors, printers, keyboards, routers and servers.

🧊 Large Appliances

Refrigerators, freezers, washing machines, air conditioners, televisions and microwaves.

Environmental & Health Hazards

When e-waste is burned or dismantled informally, toxic substances are released into the environment. According to CECIC Uganda (2026), informal dismantling can expose communities to up to 1,000 toxic chemical substances, including lead and mercury.

  • Lead — damages the nervous system, especially in children.
  • Mercury — affects the brain, kidneys and lungs.
  • Cadmium — can cause kidney and bone damage.
  • Brominated flame retardants — may disrupt hormones.

Why Informal Burning Is Dangerous

Open burning at scrap yards releases dioxins and furans — persistent pollutants that can travel long distances and contaminate soil and water sources used by nearby communities.

Safe Disposal Practices

1. Do Not Bin It

Never place batteries, phones or electronics in ordinary household rubbish. Separate them for safe collection.

2. Sanitise Your Data

Before recycling a phone or computer, back up your data, sign out of accounts, and perform a factory reset.

3. Use a Collection Point

Take items to a collection point listed on our Locations page, or book a pickup for large quantities.

🧹 Cleanliness Message

Keep your community clean and safe. Collect waste in appropriate containers and dispose of it at nearby collection points — never in drains, on roadsides or in open fields. Responsible disposal protects families, children and the environment.

📘 E-Waste Management Guidelines

Proper e-waste management has three pillars: reduce unnecessary purchases, reuse devices where possible, and recycle responsibly through verified channels. Credible disposal prevents toxic exposure and recovers valuable materials such as copper, gold and aluminium.
